      How to hunt
      1. Follow the power. Black lead on battery −, circuit on. Move red down the line. Where 12 V turns into 0 V, the open is between those two points.
      2. Voltage drop. Put red and black on both ends of one wire, connector or switch with the circuit on. A good part reads under about 0.2 V. A bigger number means resistance right there.
      3. Ground side. Black on battery −, red on the load's ground pin with the load on. More than about 0.3 V means a bad ground. One bad ground stud can kill several circuits, so look at what they share.
      4. Fuse keeps blowing. Switch to Ω (battery disconnected) and check from the fuse output to battery −. A good feed reads the load's resistance. Close to 0 Ω means a wire rubbing through to ground.
      5. Relays. Check for 12 V across pins 86 and 85 before blaming the relay. Coil reads about 85 Ω. Good coil voltage but no click means a bad relay.
      6. Module inputs. Pull-up inputs sit at 5 V with the switch open and near 0 V with it closed. The scan tool's live data shows what the module sees.
      7. Data links (CAN). Key off, Ω between CAN-H and CAN-L: 60 Ω means both 120 Ω terminators are good, 120 Ω means one is missing, near 0 Ω means a short. In this trainer both lines sit at 2.5 V with the key on (no traffic).
